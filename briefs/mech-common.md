Task family: first-principles mechanism models for Spintronics parts, plus alternative mechanisms. You own ONE mechanism (below). Work only in sim/mech/<YOURNAME>/.
Read first: notes/how-it-works.md (real kit mechanisms + official simulator models), notes/references.md, and sim/codex/ (the ideal-circuit 3D sim we keep as the reference: its solver, part conventions, visual style).
Convention: impedance analogy. Chain tension difference across a sprocket = voltage, chain speed = current. Pick one SI scaling (sprocket pitch radius r from the notes; V = torque/r, I = r·ω) and state it.
Deliverables:
1. model.js — pure ES module, no DOM. The mechanism's equations of motion derived from physics (masses, inertias, gear constraints, springs, friction, fluid shear, etc.), integrated with a stable fixed-step method. Expose a uniform port API: `create(params) -> {state, step(dt, drive) , ports}` where `drive` imposes either speed (current source) or torque (voltage source) on each external sprocket and `ports` reports the other quantity. Document every parameter with units and where its value came from (kit spec URL, measured from the notes, or "estimate" with reasoning). Never present an estimate as a spec.
2. bench.mjs — node script (node is at ~/.local/bin/node) that characterizes the mechanism as a circuit element: DC V–I sweep, step response, and small-signal frequency sweep (impedance magnitude/phase vs frequency). Fit the effective ideal element(s) (R, C, L, plus parasitics/thresholds) and print a comparison table vs the ideal part. Save results to results.json. Run it and report real numbers.
3. index.html — single self-contained page (three.js 0.160.0 via importmap from cdn.jsdelivr.net, imports ./model.js) showing the mechanism in 3D with its internals visible (cutaway/transparent housing), animated from model.js, with sliders for parameters and drive, and a small live plot of V vs I (a Lissajous/curve trace). Match sim/codex's visual style.
4. REPORT.md — derivation (equations), parameter sources, bench results, where and why it departs from the ideal element, and how to slot model.js into sim/codex as a drop-in for the ideal part. Terse.
Rules: ground physics claims in sources you open (web search is available) or in derivation; never invent constants, citations, or results. If a test fails, say so. No fallbacks that mask errors. Original code only.
