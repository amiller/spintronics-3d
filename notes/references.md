## 2. Mechanical–electrical analogies

- **A Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism, Vol. II** — James Clerk Maxwell (1873) · book · verified
  In §604 Maxwell treats currents as velocities in a dynamical account of coupled circuits. The passage shows the historical current–velocity correspondence behind the impedance analogy. [Source](https://stanslegacy.com/documents/a-treatise-on-electricity-and-magnetism-vol-2-1873-part-15)

- **An introduction to energy harvesting and micro-power management** — Aldo Romani (2018) · lecture · verified
  The slide on equivalent electromechanical circuits represents the same mass–spring–damper with a Maxwell series RLC circuit and a Firestone parallel circuit. It shows how the choice of analogy affects circuit topology. [Source](https://ssie.dei.unipd.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/SSIE18_09_Romani.pdf)

- **Bond Graphs: A Unifying Framework for Modelling of Physical Systems** — Jan F. Broenink (2020) · book chapter · verified
  The impedance analogy pairs force or torque with voltage and linear or angular velocity with current. Applying Broenink’s mapping to Upper Story’s science guide puts Spintronics in the impedance analogy because differential chain tension supplies the force and chain travel per unit time supplies the velocity. [Source](https://ris.utwente.nl/ws/files/250822857/Broenink_2020_Bond_graphs_a_unifying_framework_fo.pdf)

- **Analysis and Design of Engineering Systems** — Henry M. Paynter (1961) · book · verified
  Paynter’s MIT course notes develop bond graphs from power exchange through paired effort and flow variables. An ideal 0-junction shares effort and balances signed flows, like KCL, and a 1-junction shares flow and balances signed efforts, like KVL. [Source](https://dirac.ruc.dk/~heine/paynter/analysis_and_design_of_engineering_systems_henry_m_paynter.pdf)

- **Spintronic Junction Value Pack** — Upper Story (year unverified) · technical guide · verified
  The planetary differential enforces v_bottom = v_middle + v_top for the signed tangential speeds of the sprocket teeth. With chain speed mapped to current, that is Kirchhoff’s current law (KCL) at the junction. [Source](https://store.upperstory.com/products/spintronic-junction-value-pack)

- **Spintronic Inductor (55 H)** — Upper Story (year unverified) · technical guide · verified
  Upper Story describes momentum storage in a rotor with peripheral steel balls. The simulator pivots the rotor against a fixed frame (ref/spintronics-simulator/public/parts/inductor-part.js:64), so its flywheel is a grounded rotational inertia, the fixed-terminal case of Smith’s two-terminal inerter. [Source](https://store.upperstory.com/products/spintronic-inductor-56-h)

- **The Inerter: A Retrospective** — Malcolm C. Smith (2020) · journal article · verified
  Distinguishes the ideal inerter from hardware implementations and discusses the rotational form, where torque is proportional to relative angular acceleration between two terminals. [Source](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-control-053018-023917)

- **Synthesis of electrical and mechanical networks of restricted complexity** — Alessandro Morelli (2019) · thesis · verified
  Section 3.5 compares the impedance analogy with Firestone’s 1933 mobility analogy, detailed in the Papers section. In the topology-preserving mobility mapping, nodal force balance corresponds to KCL and the signed sum of relative velocities around a closed mechanical loop corresponds to KVL. [Source](https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/0008b1b9-9d58-4813-8e97-1db41532cc8c/content)

- **The Hydraulic Analogues of Basic Circuits: Labs for Online Learning Environments** — J. S. Bobowski (2020) · arXiv preprint · verified
  Uses hydraulic circuits to demonstrate Ohm’s law and RC charging. Flow rate serves as current and pressure difference as voltage, giving another physical analogue for the same circuit laws. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.14081)

## 4. Papers

- **A New Analogy Between Mechanical and Electrical Systems** — F. A. Firestone (1933) · journal article · verified
  Firestone’s mobility analogy maps force to current and velocity to voltage. In this mapping the mechanical network topology is preserved, and mass and spring compliance correspond to capacitance and inductance, respectively. [Source](https://www.physics.umd.edu/courses/Phys410/Anlage_Fall15/Firestone%20Electro-Mechanical%20Analogy%20Paper%20AJP%201.1915605.pdf)

- **Synthesis of Mechanical Networks: The Inerter** — Malcolm C. Smith (2002) · journal article · verified
  Defines a two-terminal element whose terminal force is proportional to relative acceleration, removing the fixed-terminal restriction of a lumped mass. Smith uses the mobility analogy for synthesis, so the ideal inerter plays the role of a capacitor in that mapping. [Source](https://www-control.eng.cam.ac.uk/foswiki/pub/Main/MalcolmSmith/cued_control_859.pdf)

- **The Differential Analyzer. A New Machine for Solving Differential Equations** — Vannevar Bush (1931) · journal article · verified
  Bush describes how reconfigurable shaft connections, mechanical integrators, and torque amplifiers solve ordinary differential equations. [Source](https://fab.cba.mit.edu/classes/862.22/notes/computation/Bush-1931.pdf)

- **Circuit-Based Design of Microfluidic Drop Networks** — Nassim Rousset, Christian Lohasz, Julia Alicia Boos, Patrick M. Misun, Fernando Cardes, Andreas Hierlemann (2022) · journal article · verified
  The hydraulic analogy maps pressure difference to voltage and volumetric flow rate to current. The paper applies it to channel resistance and extends the circuit model to nonlinear hanging and standing drops. [Source](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-666X/13/7/1124)

- **Turing Tumble is Turing-Complete** — Lenny Pitt (2021) · arXiv preprint · verified
  Proves universality by simulating a Turing machine on a hypothetical infinitely long board with unlimited pieces. The proof makes explicit which idealisations extend the physical kit. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09343)

- **The Design Principles of Konrad Zuse’s Mechanical Computers** — Raul Rojas (2016) · arXiv preprint · verified
  Describes the mechanical relays and interconnections of Zuse’s programmable binary Z1. It is an example of digital logic built from plates and rods. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.02396)

- **Mechanical transistors for logic-with-memory computing** — Huyue Chen, Chao Song, Jiahao Wu, Bihui Zou, Zhihan Zhang, An Zou, Yuljae Cho, Zhaoguang Wang, Wenming Zhang, Lei Shao, Jaehyung Ju (2023) · arXiv preprint · verified
  Uses temperature signals and a bistable mechanical actuator to combine logic and memory. It provides a comparison with Spintronics’ spring-return gate, which controls channel damping. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02352)

- **Cascading of Nanomechanical Resonator Logic** — T. Jin, C. G. Baker, E. Romero, N. P. Mauranyapin, T. M. F. Hirsch, W. P. Bowen, G. I. Harris (2022) · arXiv preprint · verified
  Models two cascaded nanomechanical NAND gates and identifies a timing constraint on transmitting the output downstream. It analyzes signal transfer between mechanical logic stages at a different scale from capstan or chain logic. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.02673)
