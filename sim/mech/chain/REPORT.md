# Chain and energy storage

The simulated 1.2 spin mF capacitor and 55 spin H flywheel ring down at **0.62141 Hz**, versus **0.61951 Hz** for ideal LC. The first resolved cycle pair gives **Q = 16.09**. Circular transmission at the reference radii gives **0.59847 Hz**. The estimated polygon geometry shifts the frequency back up, so the close agreement with ideal LC depends on the selected geometry. ## Scaling and equations

Use SI mechanical units throughout. For nominal pitch radius $r$, $q=r\theta$, $I=r\omega$ in m/s, and $V=\tau/r$ in N. Positive power enters the model, $VI=\tau\omega$. With 10 m per spin coulomb, $I_\text{spin}=I/10$, $V_\text{spin}=10V$, $C_\text{SI}=100C_\text{spin}$, and $L_\text{SI}=L_\text{spin}/100$. The simulator radii are the nominal $r$ values. [Units and radius notes](../../../notes/how-it-works.md#L5) · [Capacitor radius](https://github.com/upperstory/spintronics-simulator/blob/251395bb5acad9941f15ea4978266bbe7ee8de00/public/parts/capacitor-part.js#L4) · [Flywheel radius](https://github.com/upperstory/spintronics-simulator/blob/251395bb5acad9941f15ea4978266bbe7ee8de00/public/parts/inductor-part.js#L3) · [Motor radius](https://github.com/upperstory/spintronics-simulator/blob/251395bb5acad9941f15ea4978266bbe7ee8de00/public/parts/motor-part.js#L5)

**Polygon drive.** A tooth rotates through $2\alpha=2\pi/N$. A regular polygon of pitch $p$ has circumradius $R_p=p/(2\sin\alpha)$. Within tooth number $k$, the angle $\phi=\theta-2k\alpha$ lies in $[-\alpha,\alpha)$. Projecting the engaged pin along the span gives payout $s=kp+R_p\sin\phi$ up to an additive constant. Its derivative $a=R_p\cos\phi$ gives feed speed $a\omega$ and, by virtual work, chain torque $a(T_\text{out}-T_\text{in})$. The mean payout per radian is $\bar a=pN/(2\pi)$, and the tooth rate is $N|\omega|/(2\pi)$. The two limbs share one engagement phase per shaft. With polygonal action disabled, payout is $r\theta$. [Geometry](model.js#L51) · [Tension reactions](model.js#L236)

**Links.** Two axial lattices route around the two shafts, one per half-loop, oriented in the direction of circulation. Each has $n$ links. For displacement $x_j$ from the installed path, link extension is $e_j=x_{j+1}-x_j$. The elastic tension is $T_e=\max(0,T_0+ke_j)$. In the taut branch, $T_j=\max(0,T_e+c\dot e_j)$. Otherwise $T_j=0$. The dashpot dissipates energy because $(T_j-T_e)\dot e_j\ge0$. Newton's law gives $m\ddot x_j=T_j-T_{j-1}-d\dot x_j$. At a shaft the two adjacent half-masses total $m$, with kinetic energy $m(a\omega)^2/2$. They add $ma^2$ to shaft inertia and contribute $ma(da/d\theta)\omega^2$ to inertial effort. The shaft also carries one link's guide drag referred by $a^2$. [Link dynamics](model.js#L108) · [Shaft inertia and drag](model.js#L211)

For slow common motion, the currents are equal and the efforts add, giving $L_\text{chain}=2nm$ and $R_\text{chain}=2nd$. For differential excitation, use $I_d=I_L-I_R$ and $V_d=(V_L-V_R)/2$. With both limbs taut, differential displacement stretches one limb and relaxes the other. Each contributes $k/n$ to the differential stiffness, so $C_\text{chain}=n/(2k)$. For fixed endpoints, substitution of mode shapes $\sin(j\ell\pi/n)$ in the link equation gives first-mode frequency $f_1=\sqrt{k/m}\sin(\pi/(2n))/\pi=81.6$ Hz for the default estimates. [Link discretization](model.js#L96) · [Common and differential probes](bench.mjs#L27)

**Capacitor.** Model a flat spiral strip with small changes in curvature and section bending rigidity $EI_b$. With uniform moment and active strip length $\ell$, $\theta=\ell\Delta\kappa$ and $U=EI_b\theta^2/(2\ell)$, giving $K=EI_b/\ell=r^2/C$. A parallel spring and friction slider supply rate-independent hysteresis. Its torque state obeys $z_{n+1}=\operatorname{clip}(z_n+K_h\Delta\theta,\pm T_h)$, with slip dissipating work beyond the bound. Bearing losses add $b\omega+T_f\operatorname{sgn}\omega$, with static torque spanning $[-T_f,T_f]$ at zero speed. The maker specifies low hysteresis and a ±9 spin V rating. Strip dimensions and loss curves are unverified. [Upper Story capacitor](https://store.upperstory.com/collections/spintronic-parts/products/spintronic-capacitor-1-2-mf) · [Upper Story on stiction](https://upperstory.com/en/spintronics/troubleshooting/) · [Torque law](model.js#L132)

**Flywheel and alternative.** Rotational energy $J\omega^2/2$ gives $L=J/r^2$. Bearings follow the viscous and Coulomb law above. In the alternative, a rack or equivalent payout constrains screw translation relative to the housing to $x=r\theta$. With gear ratio $G$ and screw lead $P$, the flywheel angle is $\theta_f=(2\pi G/P)x$. Substituting into kinetic energy gives $L=J_0/r^2+J_f(2\pi G/P)^2$. The equivalent viscous loss reflects through the same squared ratio. The bench uses the housed, single-sprocket variant of a two-terminal inerter. [Upper Story flywheel](https://store.upperstory.com/collections/spintronic-parts/products/spintronic-inductor-56-h) · [Smith 2002, Fig. 3 and Eq. 1](https://www-control.eng.cam.ac.uk/foswiki/pub/Main/MalcolmSmith/cued_control_859.pdf) · [Smith, ball-screw and rack realizations](https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/0008b1b9-9d58-4813-8e97-1db41532cc8c/content) · [Transmission](model.js#L74)

**Motor.** Apply $\tau_m=rV_s$ while the spring has travel remaining and the breaker is reset. The remaining spring energy is $\tau_m\theta_\text{remaining}$. Shaft travel decrements remaining winding while engaged, with reverse travel adding winding up to capacity. At signed overspeed, the latch cuts spring torque and adds braking drag on the following tick. Rewind refills the spring and resets the latch. The physical trip speed is unverified. [Upper Story battery](https://store.upperstory.com/products/spintronic-battery-7-v) · [Breaker teardown](https://upperstory.com/en/spintronics/troubleshooting/) · [Official simulator latch](https://github.com/upperstory/spintronics-simulator/blob/251395bb5acad9941f15ea4978266bbe7ee8de00/public/parts/motor-part.js#L260) · [Model latch and winding](model.js#L256)

**Integration.** Semi-implicit Euler advances link velocities from the previous link forces and positions from the new velocities. Sprockets use the old chain loads and engagement metric. Local spring, drag, hysteresis and Coulomb terms enter a monotone backward-Euler torque law solved by its stick and slip intervals. For a linearized explicit link mode, eigenvalues of the velocity-first update give the stability condition $h^2\lambda_K+2h\lambda_D<4$. The code checks a conservative bound below 3 using the maximum inverse nodal inertia and the two incident springs per node. The release experiment also checks step refinement with tooth changes and slack active. [Step check](model.js#L185) · [Update](model.js#L236) · [Half-step run](bench.mjs#L263)

## Bench

Run `~/.local/bin/node --experimental-default-type=module sim/mech/chain/bench.mjs` from the repo root. The script sweeps signed speed at DC, records force steps, and fits the fundamental of sinusoidal voltage at prescribed current. Static displacement sweeps identify compliance. The capacitor builds angle under DC flow, so its sweep fits drag after removing spring effort. The chain uses circular engaged arms for the common and differential calibrations and subtracts bare idler inertia in AC. [Bench](bench.mjs#L38) · [Data and traces](results.json)

- **Steps.** A 0.30 N capacitor step settles to 36.051 mm at 2 s, versus 36.000 mm ideal. A 0.10 N flywheel step reaches 0.17088 m/s at 1 s versus 0.18182 m/s ideal. A 0.25 N chain step with the opposite shaft held gives 1.55994 mm at 20 s versus 1.56250 mm from the static fit. The unloaded motor trips at 0.12792 s and 156.13 rad/s. [Step traces and breaker](results.json) · [Bench drives](bench.mjs#L172)
- **Frequency.** At 1 Hz, chain common impedance is 0.03844 N·s/m at +78.74°, and differential impedance is 25.462 N·s/m at −89.99°. At 80 Hz the common impedance rises to 53.57 N·s/m near the first lattice mode. The assembled LC sweep has its sampled minimum at 0.625 Hz, 0.5385 N·s/m and −6.58°. Coulomb drag makes the fundamental impedance amplitude dependent. The sweep uses 0.01 m/s for shafts, 0.015 m/s common, and 0.0008 m/s differential. Backward integration of the shaft terms affects phase at the highest sampled frequencies. [Frequency sweeps](results.json) · [Excitations](bench.mjs#L146)
- **Chordal ripple.** At 0.18 m/s mean feed, the 20- and 13-tooth wheels give 1.236% and 2.934% peak-to-peak speed ripple at 28.125 Hz with constant shaft speeds. Both match the projected polygon to the displayed precision. [Ripple test](bench.mjs#L197) · [Results](results.json)
- **Ring-down.** To leading order, adding chain compliance to capacitor compliance and all reflected masses gives 0.59818 Hz for the circular drive. Referring the rotor inertia and spring to the mean polygon arms gives 0.62040 Hz. The full model is 0.31% above ideal, with cycle-pair Q falling from 16.09 to 14.23 as Coulomb and hysteretic work grow relative to stored energy. Ideal LC has infinite Q. Each measured pair uses the viscous-equivalent value $\sqrt{(2\pi)^2+\delta^2}/(2\delta)$ from its peak log decrement $\delta$. The default preload gives 20 slack samples at 2 ms intervals in the first 2 s. At 0.65 N preload the minimum sampled tension is 0.0407 N and the frequency is 0.62143 Hz. The inerter variant gives 0.62228 Hz and first Q of 15.93. Halving the integration step gives 0.62139 Hz and Q of 16.11. [Ring-down traces](results.json) · [Peak extraction](bench.mjs#L228)

## Reference solver integration

`step(dt, drive)` takes `{left:{speed: ω}, right:{torque: τ}}` or a per-tick callback. Omitted shafts apply zero external torque. Speed ports report the required external torque. Torque ports report the resulting speed. `ports` exposes both and their SI analogs. `chainTorque` is the internal link load used during the tick. The interval must be a positive multiple of `state.h`. [Port API](model.js#L165)

1. For a single capacitor or flywheel, create an isolated shaft with `h: STEP, right: null`. For initial spring effort $V_0$, set angle to $rV_0/K$. For a trial SI effort $v$, `companion(v)` gives current and a tangent stamp $i=Gv+j$. Use the passive branch stamp and iterate the candidate solve until the nonlinear currents agree. The preview is read-only. Commit the accepted effort with `step(STEP, {left:{torque:r*v}})`. [Model companion](model.js#L221) · [Reference stamps and acceptance](../../codex/index.html#L299)
2. Stamp the isolated motor through the passive branch row with active history. Remove its ideal-source unknown and old trip update. For the reference supply-to-return orientation, use mechanical $v=-v_\text{branch}$ and stamp $i_\text{branch}=-i_\text{mechanical}$. With spin units divide $G_\text{SI}$ by 100 and $j_\text{SI}$ by 10, and multiply voltage by 0.1 on model entry. Map the reset control to `rewind()`. In-memory reference MNA checks passed for the capacitor, flywheel, and motor with a 2 N·s/m load. [MNA source rows](../../codex/index.html#L257) · [Reference trip](../../codex/index.html#L384) · [Rewind](model.js#L204)
3. For the compliant loop, use the two-shaft model as the LC preset's solver at `step(STEP)` with the default internal substep. Use its shaft angles and link displacements in the view, with the corresponding wrap signs. A single-current ideal wire stamp would remove the independently moving chain ends.
